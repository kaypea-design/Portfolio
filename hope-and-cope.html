UX ResearchDesign

Hope & Cope Cancer Wellness Center

Research to help patients and caregivers discover Hope & Cope’s free cancer support at the right time, and feel ready to use it.

Role
UX researcher and co-presenter of research and insights
Team
10-person HEC Montréal research team
Partner
Hope & Cope, Jewish General Hospital
Methods
Literature review, benchmarking, site observation, interviews
01

Context

Hope & Cope offers free support to patients and caregivers during their cancer journey, with services and activities that help people affected by cancer improve their quality of life during a difficult time.

Patients and caregivers may not discover Hope & Cope at the right time, and even when they do, they may not feel motivated to engage with its services.

Mandate

Conduct user research that identifies the most promising opportunities and delivers multidimensional recommendations, so that everyone who could benefit from Hope & Cope can find and access it when it is most useful to them.

02

Hypotheses

H1a

Personal referral speeds up outreach

When staff, a nurse or a volunteer tells someone about support, they reach out faster than people who only see posters or online information.

H1b

Caregivers find support by accident

Caregivers usually learn about support while looking for medical information for the patient, not support for themselves.

H2a

Timing beats confusion

The wait between hearing about support and accessing it comes down to timing and readiness, not confusion or access barriers.

H2b

Personal reasons, not practical ones

People who know support exists but do not use it have personal reasons, like not wanting to identify as a cancer patient or believing others need it more.

03

Research plan

Secondary

Literature review

How patients and caregivers seek, receive and process information, and when they are cognitively ready to take it in.

Secondary

Competitive benchmarking

Montréal cancer-specific peers, including West Island Cancer Wellness Centre and Cedars CanSupport.

Primary

Site visit and observation

JGH offices compared with the Wellness Centre: navigation, atmosphere, information and who it is pitched at.

Primary

Exploratory interviews

7 participants: patients, caregivers and a physician, about how people discover, engage with or step away from support.

04

Key findings

Secondary research showed that information readiness changes throughout the cancer journey. At diagnosis many patients are in shock and “shut down”, later move to “taking it on”, then to “taking control”. Caregivers often become the information seekers when patients are overwhelmed.

Interview insights

1

Proactive beats self-discovery

Pamphlets are easily overlooked. Patients prefer to be offered support rather than having to find it.

2

The care team is trusted most

Support is far more likely to be adopted when introduced by the medical team than found online.

3

Caregivers put themselves last

Caregivers consistently deprioritize their own emotional and physical health to focus on the patient.

4

Diagnosis is overwhelming

Support is most needed at diagnosis, yet patients are too shocked to process complex service details.

5

Peers help some, burden others

Peer groups normalize the experience for many, but some patients prefer strict one-to-one care.

6

Generic feels impersonal

Broad offerings make people feel like “just a number”, leading to disengagement.

“If they have to initiate contact on their own, they often hesitate.” Physician, P02
“I was overwhelmed and worried almost all the time, but I didn’t really stop to think about my own feelings.” Caregiver, P03
“I felt like I was just a number among many others, I stayed pretty cold toward all the services being offered.” Patient, P07
05

Recommendations

1

Discover through the care team

Make it easier to discover Hope & Cope through the existing medical care team, leveraging that trust.

2

Reach out to caregivers

Proactively let caregivers know support is available for them too, not only for the patient.

3

Stay present, without pressure

Keep emotional support visible at different points after diagnosis, so people can engage when they feel ready.

4

Flexible peer connection

Offer different ways and levels of involvement to connect with peers, matched to each person’s readiness.

Next steps

  • Interview Hope & Cope patients, caregivers and volunteers.
  • Survey to identify barriers, needs and opportunities.
  • Map key user journeys and service touchpoints, then develop personas.
  • Ideate and design potential solutions.
