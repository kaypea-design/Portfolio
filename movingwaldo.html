UX/UI Design

MovingWaldo

Canada-wide movers booking platform. Three years of onboarding form optimizations and a new account settings page, helping people book movers online.

Role
Lead Product Designer
Team
1 head of product, 1 developer, 1 designer
Timeline
2021 to 2023
Platform
Website and web app
01

The problem

“We noticed drops, confusion and user errors at various points in the onboarding funnel.”

MovingWaldo is a relocation app and website for generating moving estimates. The onboarding form was where users told us about their move, and it was losing them along the way.

03

Form optimizations

2021

Showing available services

Question
How might we show the variety of services available in the form?
Finding
Users hesitated on a first screen that asked for addresses before showing any value.
Solution
A new first screen of service cards, with one preselected based on acquisition source, built to scale as services were added.
2022

Knowing where you are

Question
How might we help users understand where they are in the funnel?
Finding
Users pressed back after step 7 because nothing showed what came next, and the form ended with no review or confirmation.
Solution
A confirmation screen with previous and current addresses, pending requests, next steps and items needing special handling.
2023

Better sales estimates

Question
How might we adapt the form for more accurate sales estimates?
Finding
Sales had to call every customer for clarifying details, which did not scale.
Solution
New questions on square footage and volume to estimate movers and truck size, with address fields aligned one-to-one with the sales CRM.
04

User profile page

“I put in the wrong address, how do I change it?”

The platform gathered a lot of user information, but did not have a settings or profile page for users to review their contact information and inputs, nor was there a history of their activities. If a user entered the wrong contact information, there was nowhere to verify and update it. And since this is a platform where users request quotes for their move, there was no record for them to view which quotes they received in case they wanted to review them again.

Direction

01

Understand the expectation

We created a list of the common expectations one should be able to easily find on a settings page.

02

Use data we already have, not data to obtain

We already had all the information in a database. We just needed to decide how to bring it forward to the customer.

Expectation mapping

What would someone expect to find or do on a profile page?

Based primarily on feedback from the customer service team and commonly expected actions for a profile and settings page. Customer service previously had to make edits to customer pages in the backend admin portal. Even if the information was correctly changed, there was still nowhere for the user to find and review it.

The original page was not only difficult to find, as it was not in the header menu, but it also followed an older style of UI no longer used in the app. The only way a user could see their address inputs was if they were planning to move again and needed to edit the origin and destination address.

Version 1

Versions 2 and 3

Reworking the main page wasn’t enough. I needed to create a page that was scalable for the product roadmap. From Version 1, I broke down the major elements of the profile page into contact info, moving addresses and the authorization form (consent to third-party use of user information).

I felt that the Version 2 layout would not scale well either: if we needed to add more pages and settings, users would be scrolling a very long page to get to their destination. So I shrank the side menu, pinned it to the top left corner and gave it a selected state. We also needed to emphasize when a user still had to sign their authorization form, so I added a yellow exclamation point alert to signal that their attention was needed.

Implementing feedback from the head of product

Keeping the overall layout, with scale in mind. The head of product felt that the selected edit state on a blue background would look overwhelming if there were more fields to fill in the future. I simplified the style, making the sections clearer in one column, so that new sections would be easier to scan.

Final design for the MVP

The final account settings page consisted of four screens:

1

Moving profile

Users can edit and review their contact and moving information.

2

Requests and quotes

Every change of address or moving quote request can be viewed here. There is no option to edit, as the requests have already been sent to partner companies.

3

Past moves

For users who used the service more than once: their previous moves, requests and quotes, organized by year.

4

Security and privacy

Added for future scale: users can update their password, delete their account or select notification preferences.

Outcome

Users now had control over their information.

  • They could review their information for data accuracy and security.
  • They could manage their accounts without having to call customer service.
  • Access to their own information helps build and maintain trust and transparency, confirming how and when their information was last used.
05

Outcome

  • Clearer expectations for users about the service.
  • An easier path for the sales team to launch a new service booking feature.
  • Users can check and manage their own information without contacting customer service, building trust and transparency.
